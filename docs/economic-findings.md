# Economic findings from the reference simulator

Status: simulator results only. Nothing here is validator evidence, an audit, or a validated economic model. Unless stated, runs use the factory defaults with a 20 SOL seed and a 1B-token supply (`packages/math/src/config.ts`, `FACTORY_DEFAULTS`). Reproduce with:

```sh
pnpm install && pnpm --filter @pop/math build && pnpm sim
# outputs: docs/sim-results/*.json and docs/sim-results/SUMMARY.md
```

Every number below is copied from `docs/sim-results/SUMMARY.md` as generated by that command. If you change parameters, regenerate and update this file.

## 1. The mechanism does what it says, and only that

- One-sided flow never forms scars. Twenty consecutive 1 SOL buys leave 0.300 SOL of pending quote escrow, zero activated scar inventory and zero paired contribution (`straight-pump`).
- Opposing flow forms scars at the bins both sides crossed. Five 0.5 SOL buys followed by five sells produced 52 `ScarFormed` events, 1.35M POP + 0.035 SOL of activated scar inventory and a paired counter of 0.0369 SOL. Unmatched remainders (0.0006 SOL, 879 POP) stay pending and are reported separately (`pump-then-sell`).
- Matching uses the ceiling rule `q = ceil(b * P)`; the simulator shows `q - floor(b * P)` is 0 or 1 lamport per match.
- Every unit of inventory traces to a deposit, a swap input or a charged fee. The independent ledger reconciles with the account sums after 399 chop trades, after failed trades, after fee claims and after a buyback (`repeated-chop`, `unauthorized-claims`, `buyback-from-pop`).
- Quote reserves are not monotonic. After 200 chop rounds current quote inventory was 22.74 SOL against 20 SOL seeded, because net flow moved it; it can equally move down (`repeated-chop`, `sell-depletion`).

## 2. Graduation can be manufactured, and it is expensive

A single actor round-tripping 1 SOL (buy, then sell everything) paired about 0.0129 SOL per round trip. The 100 SOL paired target therefore needs on the order of 7,700 such round trips, roughly 7,700 SOL of gross buy volume plus the matching sells, costing about 309 SOL in fees. The actor in the run burned 9.9 SOL of 10 SOL into fees and rounding across 290 round trips while recycling 255 SOL of capital (`round-trip-actor`).

Implications:

- Graduation is proof that fees were paid at both sides of many bins. It is not proof of independent holders, organic demand, or survival. The UI copy must say so.
- The upper bound on paired quote per round trip is the smaller of the buy scar fee (1.5% of the buy) and the value of the sell scar fee (1.5% of the sell) at each bin. With a fixed 150 bps fee, maturity throughput is capped at roughly 1.5% of matched two-sided volume. Reaching 100 SOL requires at least ~6,700 SOL of matched buy volume. **If that turnover is impractical for the pilot, the maturity target or the scar fee needs recalibration in a new config version before mainnet.** The brief asked for this to be stated plainly: it is.

## 3. Launch density limits single-trade size

With 1B tokens spread uniformly over 512 bins and P0 = 20 SOL / 1B, each buy-side bin holds only ~0.039 SOL of tokens at P0 (growing 1% per bin). The sell side holds 0.3125 SOL per bin. Consequences (`launch-calibration`):

- The largest single buy that fills within the 32-bin traversal cap on a fresh market is about 1.49 SOL gross.
- The largest single sell is about 500M POP (~11.1 SOL at P0).
- A 1 SOL buy moves the cursor ~22 bins (~25% price move). Twenty 1 SOL buys moved the price from bin 0 to bin 180 (`straight-pump`).

This asymmetry is a consequence of the brief's defaults, not of the mechanism. Options for a new config version: a denser base schedule near the cursor, a smaller `binMax`, or a larger seed. The UI must offer to split larger orders into separately signed transactions and must never silently raise slippage.

## 4. Splitting a trade is bounded, not invariant

1 SOL bought as 1, 100 and 1,000 swaps produced outputs of 39,542,386,829,275 / 39,542,386,793,120 / 39,542,386,793,113 POP-atomic. The largest difference is 36,162 atomic units, 9.1e-8 % of the output, and the scar fee was identical (15,000,000 lamports) in all three cases (`split-trades`). Sources: fee flooring (at most 3 atomic units per swap across the three fee lines), per-bin rounding surplus (less than one base unit's price in the final bin of each swap), and path dependence when scars activate between splits. Do not claim exact splitting invariance.

## 5. Slippage protection works; loose tolerance is exploitable

A victim quoting a 0.5 SOL buy with 1% tolerance (after fees) reverted with `OutputBelowMinimum` after a 1 SOL front-run (`sandwich`). With a 25% tolerance the victim executed 19.1% worse than quoted and the attacker netted +0.059 SOL after paying 2% per side (`sandwich-loose`). The attacker's own fees became pending escrow at the traversed bins. The trade ticket defaults must be tight, and the UI must never raise the tolerance silently.

## 6. Historical scars are not exit liquidity

Scars formed near bins 199..201 during a pump stayed at those bins after the price was sold back down to bin 5. Sell depth within 5% of the new cursor price was 0.21 SOL across 6 bins; the historic contributions far above the price provided none of it (`distant-scar`). The three metrics (historical paired quote, current inventory, executable depth) must be displayed separately, exactly as the brief requires.

## 7. Boundaries fail closed

- Sells into an exhausted quote side revert with `SellInventoryExhausted` at bin -65; remaining quote inventory was 0 SOL and the cursor sat at -64. Failed swaps changed nothing (`sell-depletion`).
- Buys past the top bin revert with `BuyInventoryExhausted` (`buy-depletion`, TEST range).
- A swap needing an uninitialized page reverts with `PageNotInitialized`; initializing the page later recovers; re-initialization is rejected (`wrong-page`).
- No instruction mints inventory, synthesizes SOL, or provides a floor.

## 8. Graduation requires both conditions and happens once

With TEST thresholds (1 SOL paired, 2 bands at 0.01 SOL) the market graduated exactly once at trade 137; 13 later swaps paid identical fees and a keeper match pass emitted nothing new. Runs that satisfied only the paired target (3 of 40 bands) or only the band target (0.88 of 1,000 SOL) stayed `active` (`maturity-threshold`).

## 9. Seed size decides single-swap capacity (`seed-size-sweep`)

| Seed | Largest single buy (32 bins) | Largest single sell | 0.01 SOL buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 SOL | 0.0747 SOL | 500M tokens (0.5 SOL at P0) | 5 bins, +4.1% |
| 5 SOL | 0.3736 SOL | 500M tokens (2.5 SOL at P0) | 2 bins, +1.0% |
| 20 SOL | 1.4944 SOL | 500M tokens (10 SOL at P0) | 1 bin, 0.0% |

Capacity scales linearly with the seed because the base schedule is uniform over 512 bins. With the owner's 1 SOL minimum, a fresh coin cannot absorb a 0.1 SOL buy in one transaction. The launch review shows this limit; the ticket tells traders to split. This is the brief's calibration, not validated economics; a denser base schedule near the cursor is the obvious alternative for a new config version.

## 10. Markets are isolated (`multiple-launches`)

Three coins with 1 / 5 / 20 SOL seeds traded in interleaved rounds. After every round the two untouched markets' snapshots were byte-for-byte unchanged, each market reconciled, and creator fees and buyback earmarks accrued only to the market that traded (coin 1: 0.000625 SOL + 575k tokens creator claimable after 20 swaps). Sweeping the earmark moved only the fee-vault earmark; locked vaults stayed intact.

## 11. Interrupted creation resumes without duplicates (`interrupted-creation`)

A launch that stopped after page 0 resumed by creating pages −1, 1 and 2 only; a second resume created nothing; activation succeeded once and was refused the second time. A later 0.05 SOL buy from bin 40 needed page 3, which the trader created first; the virtual-page quote predicted the on-chain output exactly. The validator suite covers the same flow with real accounts.

## 12. Buybacks are escrow-only

Every market earmarks 50% of its SOL protocol fee; sweeping is permissionless; withdrawal is bounded (cap, interval, published POP mint, destination fixed to the authority's own WSOL account) and is meant to be executed in the same transaction as a Jupiter buy and an SPL burn (`services/keeper`). The simulator models the earmark and sweep; execution is off-program and shown on `/status` with "burn in same tx" per withdrawal.

## 13. What the simulator cannot show

- Compute budget, account limits, rent and transaction size: measured on the validator (see `docs/deployment.md` and the status report).
- Mint/vault/program substitution: account constraints in the Anchor program, exercised by the integration tests.
- Upgrade authority: a deployment fact, reported on `/status`, not a simulation.
- Behaviour of real wallets, RPC latency and MEV on a live network.
